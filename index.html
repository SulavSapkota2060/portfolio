Wildfire retardant drops: which side was protected?

A geospatial machine-learning pipeline that predicts which side of an aerial fire-retardant drop is the protected side, using day-of-burning rasters and the drop geometry. Research at Washington and Lee. The biggest jump came from comparing each drop's two sides across the whole fire, not just next to the drop.

  • 91.5%fire-grouped 5-fold CV (20,022 drop sides, 420 fires)
  • 89.6%on 2020–2021 drops labeled by different people
  • 68.9%for a simple rule baseline
  • Python
  • GeoPandas
  • Rasterio
  • XGBoost
  • scikit-learn

Himalayan Quake Atlas

A Python and SQLite pipeline over 10,125 USGS earthquakes (1990–2026) with an interactive map and charts. I estimate the Gutenberg-Richter b-value, and the main lesson is that the answer swings from 0.79 to 1.27 depending on the completeness cutoff you pick, so the dashboard shows the whole curve and explains the choice.

  • Python
  • SQLite
  • pandas
  • Chart.js
  • Leaflet

Rumor Mill

A C++20 discrete-event simulator of gossip spreading through a network, with a priority-queue engine, seeded randomness, message loss and latency. Across 180 runs on a 1,000-node graph, raising the fanout from 1 to 6 cut the time to reach 99% of nodes from 10.6 to 1.8, but total messages stayed around 10–11k, mostly duplicates.

  • C++20
  • Python
  • Make

Microplastics detection

Deep learning to find and classify microplastics in stereo-microscope images, at Kathmandu Institute of Applied Science. U-Net and VGG16 models reached 82% test accuracy on binary detection (783 images) and were extended to five plastic types: foam, pellet, film, fiber and fragment. YOLOv8 counted particles (0.40 mAP50). It won Best Poster at MoChWo 2024 in Kathmandu.

  • TensorFlow
  • YOLOv8
  • OpenCV

Research project, no public code

About

I'm from Kathmandu, and I now study math and computer science at Washington and Lee University in Virginia. I like problems where careful math meets messy real data: burn maps from wildfires, microscope images, and 36 years of earthquake records. I'm happiest when I can explain exactly why a number is what it is, including where it could be wrong.

Outside the lab, I co-founded Yukti Foundation, an empowerment-focused NGO in Kathmandu. Our first project installed a sanitary pad vending machine for women at Dashrath Stadium, paid for with the $280+ we raised, and we volunteer at community sports events like the SAFF Women's Tournament. I also shadow stock pitches at the Williams Investment Society, and before college I competed in chemistry and informatics olympiads and built a maze-solving robot with Arduino.
